What happened

The fourteenth-century cathedral church of St Michael was gutted by fire during Operation Moonlight Sonata, the only English cathedral destroyed in the Second World War. The tower, spire and outer walls survived.

Casualty note

No casualty figure exists for the cathedral itself, and the famous 568 must not be read as one — that is the toll for the whole city across the whole night.

Also worth knowing

The ruins were kept as a memorial beside Basil Spence's new cathedral rather than cleared — a deliberate decision to leave the damage legible.

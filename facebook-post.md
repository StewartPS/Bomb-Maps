# Facebook post — draft copy

Pick one. All of them lead with the thing that actually makes people click:
*your own street*. None of them oversell what the data is.

---

## Option 1 — the direct one (recommended)

> **Do you know what fell on your street?**
>
> I've been building a map of where the bombs landed in the Second World War.
> It's up to 236 individually researched records now — every one with a date,
> a location and the sources it came from — plus 3,043 points digitised from
> the wartime bomb census over Plymouth alone.
>
> It covers Devon and Cornwall through to London, Coventry, Liverpool, Hull
> and York, and it's just crossed into Belfast, Clydebank and Swansea.
>
> Have a look and see what happened where you live.
> 👉 bombmaps.co.uk
>
> *For archival and historical interest only — not for construction or land
> risk assessment.*

---

## Option 2 — the specific one, leading with a story

> On the night of 29 April 1942, a bomb fell on the pavement between numbers
> 37 and 39 Amberley Street in York. Fourteen people died there — nine of
> them in one house. It's a street almost nobody writes about.
>
> I've spent months plotting incidents like that onto a map: 236 researched
> records so far across 20 counties, each with its date, its sources, and an
> honest note where the historians disagree.
>
> 👉 bombmaps.co.uk

## Practical notes

- **Post the image as an image**, don't rely on the link preview. Facebook's
  preview crop is unpredictable and a native image upload gets more reach.
  Put the URL in the first line of the text.
- **Use `og-image.png` (1200×630)** for the link preview, and
  `share-square.png` (1080×1080) if you're posting to a group feed or
  Instagram, where square fills more of the screen.
- If the preview shows an old image, run the URL through Facebook's Sharing
  Debugger and hit "Scrape Again" — Facebook caches Open Graph images hard.
  https://developers.facebook.com/tools/debug/
- Local history groups are where this will actually travel. A post naming the
  specific town, with one incident from it, will outperform a generic one
  every time — Option 2 rewritten per town.
- Reply to comments with a direct record link (`bombmaps.co.uk/?record=<id>`).
  Those deep links open the map with that incident already selected.
